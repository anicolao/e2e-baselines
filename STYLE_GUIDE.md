# Writing style

Write for someone who wants to understand the decision and get on with the work.

## Say what we’ll do

- Lead with the outcome or chosen design.
- Describe the action, owner, and result.
- Use positive statements. Mention exclusions only when they prevent a likely mistake or explain a real tradeoff.
- Mention only the projects and chosen options involved in the work.
- State each rule once. Put shared rules in one place and link to them.

## Use fewer words

- Use plain words, active verbs, and a conversational tone.
- Explain how something works, then connect it to the benefit. Prefer “we check the manifest, so we can discard matching screenshots” to separate statements about storage and verification.
- Brevity means fewer ideas to untangle, not just fewer words. Keep connecting words that make the reasoning easy to follow.
- Prefer “we’ll store images” to “the system will provide image storage capabilities.”
- Cut introductions, throat-clearing, obvious consequences, and repeated caveats.
- Keep paragraphs short and focused on one idea.
- Remove any sentence the reader can infer from the previous one.

## Keep the useful detail

- Include details that affect how we build, test, review, or approve the work.
- Explain a constraint where it affects a decision.
- Distinguish measured facts, estimates, and targets.
- Keep essential risks concrete: the failure, its effect, and how we’ll handle it.
- Describe proposals as plans and completed work as results.

## Make documents easy to use

- Use short headings for substantial sections.
- Use numbered lists for sequences and bullets for parallel items.
- Use tables for comparisons, mappings, and test cases.
- Show a small example when it explains more quickly than prose.
- Link to authoritative configuration instead of copying details that will drift.
- Keep acceptance criteria measurable and close to the work they cover.

## Edit before saving

Ask of every paragraph: does this help the reader decide, build, test, or review? Keep the answer and cut the rest. Check links, examples, and consistency after shortening.
